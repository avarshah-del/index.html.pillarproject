Ages 9–12 · Core Book Agents

The mission briefing for the readers the book was built for

The book's own page calls this a classified briefing for ages 8–12. Readers here can hold both layers at once: Agent H.M.'s spy plot, and the glossary underneath it. Big Boss, the Word Warehouse manager, the Emotion-Land rides, and the Head Librarian are still characters. They are also the prefrontal cortex, Broca's and Wernicke's areas, the amygdala, and the hippocampus.

Page ranges, when they appear, are the stops published on the Under 5 guide. Nothing here diagnoses Alzheimer's or promises what any one person will remember.

Step 1 · After a full read, or zone by zone

Questions with more than one good answer

These are discussions, not the Training Zone's multiple-choice security checks. Let a reader disagree, as long as they can point at the story. The four phases below are the same path the sponsor briefing uses: Brain HQ, the Word Warehouse, Emotion-Land, then the Data Library.

Phase 1 · Brain HQ · prefrontal cortex

Why does Big Boss panic about a cookie recipe?

The Double-Chocolate-Chunk Cookie recipe is an ordinary plan, and ordinary plans live in Brain HQ, behind the forehead. Losing "what's for dinner" or a grandchild's name in the moment can look small from outside and enormous inside the office that is supposed to keep the day organized. Ask: which everyday file in your own week would scare you if it vanished?

Phase 2 · Word Warehouse · Broca's & Wernicke's areas

The voice is huge. The manager is tiny. What do you do with that?

Tangles wrap the glowing word-orbs and the book names the result a Class-8 aphasia: trouble finding words, not a monster and not a lack of thoughts. A sharp or stuck voice can be the glitch getting loud. The Training Zone pledge is explicit: never tease, and do not get frustrated, when someone forgets a word. What would "offering a word" look like compared with "finishing every sentence"?

The ride between zones · signal scooter

Retell the scooter jump in glossary words.

H.M. jumps the synaptic gap. The launch side is the axon terminal. The catchers on the far side are dendrites. The blue bubbles are neurotransmitters. Myelin, earlier on the Synaptic Express, was the speed booster around the action potential. ATP is the fuel that runs out — "BEEP! SPUTTER... PFFFT!" Use those six terms in order, in your own sentence. Then say which part of that ride is a metaphor and which part is the real cell biology.

Phase 3 · Emotion-Land · amygdala

The rides are out of whack. Are the feelings fake?

The Training Zone's Ride Operator says the park of feelings is glitching, and the quiz marks this sentence true: even if someone forgets things, their feelings are still real. A calm voice and a comforting hug are the "gadget" the zone accepts. A loud whistle and running away are not. Where would you rather be if you were the person on the weedy roller coaster?

Phase 4 · Data Library · hippocampus

What does ZAP actually do?

The New Data section is blocked by weeds, so new memories are the ones that fail to save. The Deep Archives — long-term memory — still hold older favorites, and the golden screens play them. ZAP does not rip the weeds out. If you were briefing a younger sibling, how would you explain the button so they do not expect the disease to switch off?

After the book · the pledge

Which pledge line is the hardest to keep?

The Agent Training Zone asks readers to check four promises: patience and kindness; no teasing when a word or memory drops; comfort when the amygdala is overwhelmed; and remembering the person is still there when the brain glitches. Pick the one you would most want a friend to keep with your family, and the one you might break on a bad day. That second answer is the useful one.

Step 2 · Glossary, used as science

Memory, neurons, and the words the book already defined

Definitions below are the ones in Agent H.M.'s glossary and the Training Zone, plus two careful notes where a family will hear a word the plot does not act out. This is general, well-established background. It is not a description of any one person's brain.

How a message moves

  1. 1Action potential. A high-speed zap of electricity shoots down the neuron. The book puts Agent H.M. on that zap and calls the ride the Synaptic Express.
  2. 2Myelin. A wrapping that lets the message zoom. Speed boosters on the train.
  3. 3Axon terminal and dendrites. The launch pad where the message jumps off, and the catchers that receive it. Neurons do not touch.
  4. 4Synaptic gap and neurotransmitters. The tiny empty space, and the chemical messengers — the blue bubbles — that cross it.
  5. 5ATP. The energy that powers cells. In the story the Signal Scooter dies when the fuel runs out. Running out of story-fuel is a picture of a tired system, not a set of instructions for fixing one.

The Data Library

The hippocampus is the memory saver that grabs new memories and files them. The Training Zone describes its shape as a tiny seahorse, and its security question says that when Alzheimer's affects this library, what usually gets forgotten first is something new — not a childhood name, and not how to breathe. The book's Deep Archives are the long-term vault of older favorites. Photos and old songs are the visit the Training Zone recommends. They are not a cure, and the story does not promise every old memory stays forever.

Tangles, and the word "plaques"

Tangles, in the glossary, are twisted proteins that block tracks like weeds. That is the picture the plot actually shows: vines on word-orbs, weeds on the roller coaster, weeds across the New Data door. The book page also says readers learn what happens when plaques and tangles appear. Plaques are a second, well-known change in Alzheimer's — clumps that build up between cells. The guides on this site do not describe a plaque scene, because the pages we have do not narrate one. Do not add a monster that the book did not draw.

Cleanup is not a reset button

The proteasome unit is the glossary's clean-up crew, the part that clears garbage. Microglia, on the home page, are glial cells — immune security agents that help keep neurons healthy — and on this website Microglia is the guide you can ask. Neither one erases the weeds in the story. If you spot the proteasome while you read, name it. Do not invent a chapter where the crew wins and the disease leaves.

Use the tools that are actually live

The classified code that opens the Book Protocol is printed in the book, not on this page. Enter it yourself in the Agent Training Zone and reboot the four zones. For a real visit with an older person, the HQ Rec Room currently has one ready mission, Ping-Pong Color Catch. Musical Memory Lane and Tactile Art are still marked "mission loading."
