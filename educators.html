A classroom companion, separate from the family age router

Teach the story. Do not teach a diagnosis.

Case Alzheimer's is informational fiction: a spy mission through Brain HQ that lines up with real words for neurons, memory, language, and feelings. In a classroom it works as a read-aloud plus a printable, not as a unit on anyone's grandparent. The sponsor briefing describes a school pilot for 4th, 5th, and 6th grade science in Livingston, New Jersey, with the book and a neuroscience author visit. This page is the zero-prep flow for that kind of visit, and for any other room that has the book.

Lesson flow All four guides

One period, about 45–60 minutes

Lesson flow

For 4th–6th grade, use the Core Book Agents guide and its Four Zones mission file. Younger read-alouds use Early Readers or Under 5. A middle or high school group that is talking about caregiving, not just cells, uses Teens & Youth Allies. The book's own briefing labels the core read as ages 8–12.

  1. 01 · Open · about 5 minutes

    Forehead, then one pair of words

    Everyone taps their forehead: that is Brain HQ, the prefrontal cortex, "the boss of the brain." Put one glossary pair on the board from the Parent Guide — book name on the left, science name on the right. Tell the room the story is about Alzheimer's, which is a disease, and that no one will be asked to share a family story.

  2. 02 · Read · about 20 minutes

    Four phases, one pause each

    Read aloud, or have students share the short shouted lines if you are with younger readers. Pause once in each phase the sponsor briefing already names: Brain HQ (the missing Double-Chocolate-Chunk Cookie recipe and the tangles), the Word Warehouse (Class-8 aphasia, tangles on the word-orbs, a tiny manager with a huge voice), Emotion-Land (weedy rides, the synaptic-gap scooter), and the Data Library (New Data blocked, Deep Archives still playing after ZAP). The Under 5 guide is the only place this site maps those scenes to page numbers. Use those ranges if your copy matches; if a printing differs, follow the place names instead of forcing a page.

  3. 03 · File it · about 15 minutes

    The printable that matches the room

    Hand out the sheet linked below. Students write or draw. You are listening for the idea that ZAP does not remove the weeds, and that a kind response is different from a quiz. Collect the sheets or let students take them home. Do not grade anyone on a personal caregiving disclosure.

  4. 04 · Close · about 5 minutes

    The Training Zone pledge, said out loud

    Read the four promises already written in the Agent Training Zone: treat people who have memory loss with patience and kindness; never tease when a word or a memory drops; show comfort when the amygdala is overwhelmed; remember the person is still there when the brain glitches. Stop there. If students have their own copy, the classified code in the back of the book opens the online Book Protocol later. Do not put that code on a slide.

Say this

Alzheimer's is a disease that can hide new memories, scramble word-finding, and make feelings surge. The person is still there. Patience is the assignment. The science words are the ones in the glossary, used as the book uses them.

Leave this out

No student medical histories. No claim that a hug, a game, or a class period treats or reverses the disease. No secret book code on a shared screen. No unfinished Rec Room activities: only Ping-Pong Color Catch is ready. Musical Memory Lane and Tactile Art are still loading.
