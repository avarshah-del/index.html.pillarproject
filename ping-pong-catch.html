Ping-Pong Color Catch

A gentle, face-to-face game using bright cups and a rolling ping-pong ball to create a joyful, non-verbal connection.

📋 The Physical Interaction (Step-by-Step)

1

Sitting Face-to-Face

The child and the adult sit directly across from each other at a small table, close enough to easily see each other's expressions. Each person has their two differently colored cups (e.g., Red in the left hand, Blue in the right hand).

2

The Child Starts

The child takes the ping-pong ball and holds it up, making sure they have the adult's attention. With a gentle push, the child rolls the ball across the table toward the adult.

3

The Adult Tracks and Catches

The adult watches the ball roll closer. Guided by the bright, contrasting colors of their cups, they slide either the Red or Blue cup over to trap the ball.

4

The Return Roll

Once caught, the adult tips the cup forward, releasing the ball to roll it straight back to the child. The child uses their own matching colored cups to catch it, keeping the loop going.

✨ What Makes It Special

😂 Laughter Over Rules

Children naturally bring a sense of playfulness. If the ball rolls off the table or misses a cup, a child will usually laugh and chase after it. This completely removes any pressure or feelings of "failure" for the adult with dementia, turning mistakes into lighthearted moments.

🎯 Simple Visual Communication

Because dementia can make verbal conversations confusing, the game replaces words with action. The child can easily prompt the adult simply by pointing to a cup or calling out a color ("Catch it with Red, Grandma!"). This gives the adult a clear, achievable goal.

👀 The Power of Eye Contact

Every time the ball rolls back and forth, both players naturally look up at each other to see the reaction. This creates a powerful, non-verbal connection, allowing the child and the family member to share a meaningful moment of focus and joy without needing complex conversation.
