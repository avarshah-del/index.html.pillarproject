For the four brain zones

Sources

Case Alzheimer's is a story. The Agent Training Zone uses real names for four brain areas, and for aphasia and Alzheimer's. These links are the public pages behind each zone. They are for teachers and families. They are not a diagnosis, and they do not say the book treats the disease.

Start here

  • Alzheimer's Disease Fact Sheet — National Institute on Aging. What the disease is, plaques and tangles, and why new memories are often affected first. The fact sheet names the hippocampus.
  • What is Alzheimer's Disease? — Alzheimer's Association.
  • Alzheimer's Disease — NIH MedlinePlus.

Brain HQ · prefrontal cortex

The zone teaches that this part sits behind the forehead, makes decisions, and solves puzzles, and that Alzheimer's is the condition behind the tangles.

  • What Happens to the Brain in Alzheimer's Disease — National Institute on Aging. Describes plaques, tangles, early damage in memory areas, and later damage in the cortex that handles reasoning and language.
  • Brain components — MedlinePlus medical encyclopedia video. The frontal lobe sits at the front of the brain.
  • Inside the Brain: Take the Brain Tour — Alzheimer's Association. The cortex section covers thoughts, problem solving, and planning.

Word Warehouse · Broca's and Wernicke's areas

The zone teaches that these areas build words and talk, that aphasia is the word-finding glitch, and that a missed word should be met with patience.

  • Aphasia — NIH MedlinePlus. Aphasia is trouble using or understanding words.
  • What is aphasia? — NIH MedlinePlus Magazine. Names Broca's area and Wernicke's area as language regions.
  • Communicating With Someone Who Has Alzheimer's Disease — National Institute on Aging. Word-finding gets harder. The advice is patience, not quizzing.
  • Communication — Alzheimer's Association.

Emotion Land · amygdala

The zone teaches that the amygdala runs feelings, that gentle words and comfort help when someone is scared, and that feelings stay real.

  • Limbic system — MedlinePlus medical encyclopedia image. Names the amygdala in emotion and behavior, and the hippocampus in memory.
  • Alzheimer's Caregiving: Managing Personality and Behavior Changes — National Institute on Aging. Fear, anxiety, and the advice to reassure the person instead of arguing.
  • Anxiety and agitation — Alzheimer's Association.

Data Library · hippocampus

The zone teaches that the hippocampus is the memory vault, shaped like a seahorse, that recent memories usually slip first, and that photos and old songs help.

  • Alzheimer's Disease Fact Sheet — National Institute on Aging. Damage often begins in the hippocampus, a part of the brain needed to form memories. Recent memory trouble is a common early sign.
  • What Happens to the Brain in Alzheimer's Disease — National Institute on Aging. The same early change in the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex.
  • Limbic system — MedlinePlus. The hippocampus is part of forming long-term memory.
  • Memory loss and confusion — Alzheimer's Association. One suggestion on that page is to show photos and other reminders.
